# AI-Driven Development Lifecycle for Financial Services

## Why the AWS source matters

This AWS for Industries post explains AI-Driven Development Lifecycle (AI-DLC) for financial services: a way to use AI agents across software delivery while keeping human review, governance, auditability, and regulatory control.

## Key points

- AI-DLC is positioned between fully autonomous AI-managed development and narrow AI-assisted coding tools.
- The method uses AI agents to help plan, code, test, document, generate infrastructure, and support operations.
- Humans remain accountable for review, validation, refinement, and approval.
- For financial services, the article emphasizes governance, traceability, risk management, and audit-ready controls.
- The workflow is organized around inception, construction, and operations-style lifecycle stages.

## Why the LCMH source matters

The LCMH article broadens the same AI-DLC topic beyond the financial-services-specific framing. It treats AI-DLC as a general software-development methodology where agentic AI can initiate workflows, decompose tasks, and propose action plans while humans validate direction, quality, and risk.

## Additional points from LCMH

- AI-DLC is framed as a full-lifecycle transformation, not just faster code generation.
- The developer role shifts from executor toward decision-maker, reviewer, validator, and risk owner.
- Different work should receive different levels of process depth; small fixes and major architecture changes should not follow the same workflow.
- Productivity metrics such as lines of code and velocity can miss whether AI-DLC produces real business value.
- Reported case studies are useful, but they should be read alongside the engineering practices that make AI-generated work reviewable: clear specs, modularity, strict review, and human accountability.
